How to read this

One creator, one cohort. Every creator sits in the cohort of the month of their first sample, so nobody is counted twice and the table adds up to the real total. (Putting a creator in every month they were sampled inflated the same total from £1.00m to £1.61m, which is why it is not done that way.)

The columns are months after the sample. M+0 is the sample month itself, M+1 the month after, and so on; M+6 and later gathers everything from the sixth month onward. Pick a sample month above and the column headers show the actual calendar months, so 2026-03 reads Mar, Apr, May and so on.

M+0 only counts what came after the box. In the sample month, only videos posted on or after the sample went out count, so revenue a creator was already making is never credited to the sample.

Revenue means VAHDAM video revenue, attributed to the month the video was posted. That is the only attribution the year-long creative export carries. Video GMV lands mostly in the days after posting, so month-of-post sits close to month-of-order but is not identical. It is our revenue from their content, not their total sales.

GMV slab is the creator's size, not our revenue. It is their current trailing-30-day GMV across every shop, from Reacher or the Affiliate Centre. It is also a live figure, so a creator who has grown since being sampled shows in today's band, not the one they were in on the day.

Young cohorts have empty columns, and that is not a failure. A cohort sampled last month has no M+3 yet. The £-per-creator view across all months therefore uses only cohorts old enough to have filled every bucket; the one-month view shows whatever that cohort has had time to earn, and the KPI row says how many months of history it has.

Sample orders here are not the Monthly sample report's sample orders. That table counts every order placed in the month. This one counts only the orders that put a creator into the cohort, so it excludes orders to creators already sampled earlier and orders with no username attached. The line above the table shows the split for the month you have selected, and the three parts add back to that table's figure exactly.

Revenue back ties to the Monthly sample report's Video GMV. Both now mean the same thing: revenue from videos the cohort posted on or after their sample. Until 24 Sep 2026 that column summed the cohort's entire video history including videos posted before we sampled them, which credited sampling with revenue it did not create; it no longer does, so for June it reads £44,669 in both places rather than £95,697 in one and £44,669 in the other.

Posted and Earned are different counts. The Monthly sample report's Posted is how many of the cohort put out any video after their sample. Earned anything here is how many produced revenue. For June that is 2,100 who posted and 313 who sold, so most posts generate nothing.

Ad spend is TikTok's product-ad cost on those same videos. Same rows, same attribution as the revenue beside it. TikTok's algorithm decides how budget lands on each creative; we supply the budget, so this is reported exactly as spent and never modelled back from performance.

Sample cost is the COGS of the goods we posted, priced from the pack-cost table, and bucketed by the month the sample order went out. M+0 is the samples that created the cohort; later buckets are re-samples to the same creators. Only an order carrying a username can be costed to a creator, so a redacted order's goods sit outside this.

Two ROAS columns, and they disagree on purpose. ROAS is revenue divided by ad spend alone, which is ad efficiency. Final ROAS divides by ad spend plus sample cost, which is what the cohort actually cost us. Anything under 1.00 is shown in red: the cohort returned less than it consumed. The two diverge most in the low slabs, where TikTok spends almost nothing but we still post a box — so ad ROAS flatters them and final ROAS does not.

The slab ticks filter everything below them. The table, the Selected row, the chart and the creator list all respect them, so ticking only £1K+ gives you that segment's own revenue, spend and ROAS rather than a share of the whole. Selected recomputes its ROAS from the selected slabs' own revenue and spend; it is not an average of the rows above it.

The creator table lists everyone who earned anything, plus anyone with at least £1 of ad spend. Most sampled creators never post or never sell, so listing all of them would be thousands of zero rows; those with neither revenue nor spend are left out. The counts are in the KPI row, and ↓ Every creator above downloads all of them including the zeros.
